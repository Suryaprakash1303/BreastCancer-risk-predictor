Wisconsin Breast Cancer Diagnostic Dataset Details

Information on feature extraction and how cell nuclei properties are measured.

Features are computed from a digitized image of a fine needle aspirate (FNA) of a breast mass. They describe characteristics of the cell nuclei present in the image.

Feature Interpretations:

  • Radius: Mean of distances from center to points on the perimeter of the cell nucleus.
  • Texture: Standard deviation of gray-scale values (roughness vs. smoothness of the cell texture).
  • Perimeter & Area: Measures size of the cell nucleus, which is typically larger and more irregular in malignant cells.
  • Smoothness: Local variation in radius lengths.
  • Compactness: Computed as perimeter² / (area - 1.0). Indicates how close to a perfect circle the cell is.
  • Concavity: Severity of concave portions of the nucleus contour.
  • Concave Points: Number of concave portions of the contour.
  • Symmetry: Measures cell symmetry, often disrupted in cancer cells.
  • Fractal Dimension: Measures the complexity of the nuclear boundary ("coastline approximation").

Analysis Methodology:

This UI uses two trained models. In the original notebook, models are standardized and evaluated. When running the predictions in real time:

  1. Your input parameters are collected from the sliders.
  2. They are normalized using standard scaling: z = (x - mean) / std. The means and standard deviations are computed from the training dataset.
  3. The dot product is taken between the standardized inputs and model coefficients, plus the intercept: score = w · z + b.
  4. For Logistic Regression, the score is put through a Sigmoid activation: p = 1 / (1 + e^-score) to get a probability.
  5. For SVM, the distance to the linear decision hyperplane is converted to a probability score.
